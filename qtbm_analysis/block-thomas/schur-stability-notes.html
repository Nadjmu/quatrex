Block Thomas · stability note

The two Schur-complement panels

What the schur_growth figure shows, and why those two quantities — not the scalar growth factor — decide whether block Thomas is backward stable. Self-contained.

§ 1

Setup

M is block tridiagonal with N diagonal blocks A11, …, ANN, sub-diagonal blocks Ak+1,k and super-diagonal blocks Ak,k+1. Every block is m × m, where m is the number of orbitals in one layer of the device. Making the device longer increases N; it does not increase m.

Block LU — the “block Thomas” recursion — computes

S1 = A11 Sk = Akk − Ak,k−1 Sk−1−1 Ak−1,k k = 2, …, N

The Sk are the pivot blocks. They are exactly the diagonal blocks of the U factor in M = LU, and the code stores them as Dmod. Two implementations differ only in how they use Sk:

  • LU-based: factor Sk = Pk Lk Uk once, with partial pivoting inside the m × m block, and reuse it for every solve against Sk.
  • inverse-based: form Gk = Sk−1 explicitly.

Both need to solve linear systems with Sk as the coefficient matrix, in two places:

  1. forming Sk+1 needs Sk−1 Ak−1,k, i.e. solve Sk Y = Ak−1,k;
  2. the substitution phase solves Sk z = (right-hand side) for each block.
§ 2

Why block LU is not scalar LU

Scalar LU with partial pivoting is backward stable in practice because the multipliers are bounded, |Lij| ≤ 1, so the only thing that can go wrong is element growth in U, measured by the growth factor ρ = max|U| / max|M|. If ρ is modest, ‖M − LU‖ ≤ γn ‖|L||U|‖ is small and the solve is backward stable.

Block LU replaces “divide by the scalar pivot” with “solve a system with the block pivot Sk”. That step is backward stable in the usual sense — the computed solution ŷ satisfies (Sk + δSk) ŷ = c with ‖δSk‖ / ‖Sk‖ ∼ u — but its forward error is

‖ŷ − y‖ / ‖y‖ ≲ κ2(Sk) · u κ2(Sk) = ‖Sk‖ ‖Sk−1‖

That inexact ŷ is then substituted into Sk+1, into the L multipliers, and into the solution blocks, and the error propagates down the recursion. The result (Demmel, Higham & Nichols, Block LU factorization is not always stable, 1995) is that the backward error of the whole block factorization is bounded by roughly

‖M − L̂ Û‖ / ‖M‖ ≲ c(N, m) · ( 1 + maxk κ2(Sk) ) · u

The maxk κ2(Sk) factor has no counterpart in the scalar partial-pivoting bound. Block LU is backward stable iff the pivot blocks stay well conditioned — the growth factor alone does not certify it.

This is a different κ from the one in the forward-error bound. κ(M) governs how far x is from the true solution given a backward-stable solve. κ2(Sk) is an internal quantity of the block algorithm that degrades the backward error itself. A well-conditioned M can still produce an ill-conditioned Sk: Sk is the Schur complement of the leading k−1 block rows, and it is near singular whenever that leading principal submatrix is near singular, independently of κ(M).

§ 3

The two panels

Both are 2-norm quantities, one SVD per block per energy index. Both implementations share the Sk exactly, so their curves coincide; only one is plotted.

Panel 1 — block growth maxk ‖Sk‖2 / maxk ‖Akk‖2

The block analogue of ρ. Did the recursion inflate the blocks relative to the diagonal blocks of M it started from? A value near 1 means the Sk are no larger than the Akk; a large value means the subtraction Akk − Ak,k−1 Sk−1−1 Ak−1,k produced a much bigger block, which feeds element growth into U.

Panel 2 — pivot conditioning maxk κ2(Sk)

The quantity from § 2. This is the one that enters the block LU backward error where scalar LU has nothing. Even with block growth of 1, a single near-singular Sk sends this over 105 or 106 and costs that many digits in the block solves against Sk — and hence in the assembled factors, at the working precision u.

For the QTBM matrices M(E) = E I − H − Σ(E) this spikes at the band edges: M(E) itself approaches singularity there (the resolvent blows up near an eigenvalue), and that near singularity concentrates into one Schur complement. Away from the band edges κ2(Sk) sits at 102–103 and block LU is uniformly backward stable.

§ 4

Reading them with the other figures

  • backward_error figure. At complex128, κ2(Sk) ∼ 106 times u ∼ 10−16 is still ∼ 10−10 ≪ 1, so ω stays at u even at the band edge. At complex64, u ∼ 6×10−8, the same product is ∼ 6×10−2, and the block Thomas ω visibly bumps up at the conduction edge — this panel is the explanation for that bump.
  • growth_factor figure, assembly-residual panel. ‖Meff − LU‖ / ‖Meff‖ tracks (block growth) × (pivot conditioning) × u. At complex128 it is ∼ 10−15 everywhere; at complex64 it rises to ∼ 10−2 at the band edge, which is κ2(Sk) · u again.
§ 5

Scalability caveat

κ2(Sk) is computed here by a full SVD of each m × m block: O(N m3), linear in N, the same order as the factorization. It does not blow up as the device lengthens. If m itself is large, the SVD is replaceable by a 1-norm condition estimate (LAPACK ?gecon) reusing the pivot-block LU that implementation 1 already computes — O(m2) per block. For a post-hoc, strided analysis over two materials the SVD is fine and gives the exact number.
